# DUMA PAY — Global Financial Platform Roadmap

**Product vision:** Build an inclusive, interoperable payments and commerce platform designed in South Africa and expandable across Africa and approved international corridors.

**Brand promise:** YOUR MONEY. YOUR CHOICE.

**Current delivery boundary:** DUMA PAY's current Android app is a demonstration prototype. Its wallet balance, payment methods, checkout, QR screens, transfers, vouchers, merchant tools and support drafts do not move real money. No bank, card scheme, remittance provider, government department or payment network is represented as a signed DUMA partner.

## 1. Product principles

1. **One clear experience:** A person should be able to receive, pay, send, organise and understand money from one DUMA interface.
2. **Interoperability over isolation:** Connect to banks, payment schemes, card issuers, merchants, cash networks and remittance providers through approved interfaces.
3. **Cash-aware and mobile-first:** Design for cash users, feature phones, low data, rural connectivity and small merchants as well as smartphone users.
4. **Transparent pricing:** Before a real transaction, show the fee, exchange rate, recipient amount, timing, limits and refund conditions.
5. **Trust before growth:** Strong identity checks, authentication, fraud monitoring, privacy, dispute handling and operational resilience are core product features.
6. **Partner-led regulated activity:** DUMA should own its product, customer experience and brand while appropriately authorised partners perform regulated activities until DUMA has the required permissions itself.

## 2. Product inspiration — learn, do not copy

| Inspiration | Useful pattern | DUMA direction |
|---|---|---|
| PayPal | Simple online checkout, payment identity, merchant acceptance, buyer/seller issue workflows | DUMA Checkout, DUMA.Me, merchant tools and dispute support |
| Wise | Clear international transfer pricing and recipient amount | DUMA Send Abroad must show a real provider quote and all fees before confirmation |
| Stripe | Developer-friendly merchant payment tools, checkout and payment links | DUMA merchant APIs, checkout SDK and business dashboard |
| Revolut | Money organisation, cards, spending controls and travel-oriented money tools | DUMA Pockets, card controls and future multi-currency views |
| Cash App / Venmo | Simple person-to-person payments, handles and split expenses | DUMA.Me, request money and split bills |
| Apple Pay / Google Pay | Tokenised credentials and fast device-based payment | Future tokenised wallet/card provisioning through certified issuers and device partners |
| M-Pesa and agent networks | Cash-in/cash-out reach and accessible payments | DUMA Cash Network with verified, contracted agents |
| Alipay / WeChat Pay | Merchant QR acceptance and integrated commerce | Interoperable merchant QR and DUMA commerce tools |
| MoneyGram / Western Union | International cash and digital remittance corridors | Partner-led inbound and outbound remittance, only where each corridor is approved |

These are product references, not endorsements, partnerships or claims that DUMA has access to their systems or intellectual property.

## 3. DUMA product pillars

### A. DUMA Financial Network
- Wallet account and transaction history
- Receive money and request money
- Person-to-person transfers using a DUMA handle, phone alias or approved proxy
- Bank transfers and instant payments through connected providers
- QR and merchant payments
- DUMA debit/prepaid card programme through an issuer
- Virtual card and tokenised device payments where issuer support allows
- Cash-in and cash-out through approved agents
- Refunds, reversals and payment status tracking

### B. DUMA Checkout and Commerce
- DUMA Checkout for online and in-person purchases
- Payment links, invoices and merchant QR
- Merchant dashboard, settlements and reconciliation
- Stock, customer, sales and expense tools
- DUMA My Change for merchant-funded change credit after confirmed settlement
- Digital receipts and refund workflow
- Subscriptions and recurring payment mandates with customer consent
- Rewards funded by participating merchants
- APIs and plugins for online stores and point-of-sale providers

### C. DUMA Global
- DUMA Send Abroad for approved outbound corridors
- Inbound remittance options through approved partners
- Potential MoneyGram and Western Union partner pathways
- Transparent FX quotes, fees, recipient amount and delivery estimates
- Multi-currency display and eligible currency balances only where legally and operationally supported
- Country-by-country limits, identity checks, sanctions screening and transaction monitoring

### D. DUMA Access
- Airtime, data, electricity and supported bill payments
- Eligible voucher redemption only where the voucher issuer permits it
- DUMA Cash Network with verified cash agents
- DUMA Lite for low-data and feature-phone access, potentially USSD through a mobile-network partner
- Assisted merchant and community access
- Accessibility and local-language support

### E. DUMA Money Management
- Money Pockets and budgeting
- Savings goals and optional partner-held savings products
- Round-Up savings only with explicit mandate and partner custody
- Family budgets and allowances with permissions
- Financial education and cash-flow insights
- Responsible credit referrals only through registered credit providers and affordability checks

### G. DUMA Stockvel
- Digital group setup for savings clubs, grocery stockvels, rotating payout circles, burial/mutual-support groups and business savings clubs
- Member directory, agreed rules, contribution targets and payment schedules
- Contribution ledger with references and reconciliation against verified bank/provider statements
- Transparent payout calendar, approval workflow and member notifications
- Group statements, meeting minutes, member permissions and dispute records
- Future bank/partner-linked group accounts with suitable mandates, signatories and withdrawal controls
- Optional merchant redemption for grocery stockvels through contracted merchants

DUMA Stockvel should begin as a record-keeping and planning tool. The current prototype stores group details, members, contribution entries and payout plans locally; it does not hold, pool, invest or transfer group money and a user-entered contribution is not proof of payment. Any live custody, pooling, investment, payment collection or payout service requires legal review, suitable authorised partners, clear ownership and mandate arrangements, safeguarding, reconciliation, fraud controls, member consent and dispute procedures. A rotating payout group must have transparent rules and must not be presented as guaranteed investment returns.

### F. DUMA Invest
- Mobile-first access to listed shares and investment products for customers across South Africa
- Company discovery, education and watchlists
- Brokerage order entry and execution through an authorised investment partner
- Share custody and investor records through the approved custody model
- Portfolio values, transaction history, dividends and corporate-action notifications
- Clear brokerage, platform, market and FX fees before an order is confirmed
- Fractional-share access only if the brokerage, exchange rules and custody provider support it
- Risk education and suitability/appropriateness checks as required by the product and law
- No guaranteed returns, misleading performance claims or unlicensed personalised investment advice

DUMA Invest should be positioned as an accessible investment front end, not as a stock exchange or broker unless DUMA obtains the necessary permissions. Live investing requires an appropriately authorised financial-services provider/broker, custody and settlement arrangements, verified investor onboarding, market-data rights, disclosures, complaint handling and regulatory review. The current app contains only a local watchlist and non-binding investment-interest drafts; it does not quote live prices or place trades.

### F. DUMA Trust and Protection
- Secure login, device binding and risk-based authentication
- Transaction notifications and customer-set limits
- Fraud detection and suspicious activity monitoring
- Disputes, chargeback support and merchant refund tracking
- Privacy controls, data access and deletion processes
- Customer support, complaints escalation and service status
- Clear receipts and audit trails

### G. DUMA Public and Business Infrastructure
- SARS and CIPC guidance and official-channel hand-off
- Future government disbursement and collection use cases
- SME invoicing, records and reporting
- Partner APIs for banks, fintechs, merchants and public institutions
- Interoperable payment infrastructure contributions where approved

The Global Payments hub adds:

## 5. South Africa-first launch sequence

### Phase 0 — Prototype and validation
- Keep the app clearly marked as a preview.
- Test flows with consumers and small merchants.
- Validate the biggest initial problem: a tightly scoped merchant payment/change or checkout use case.
- Measure task completion, trust, usability, connectivity and willingness to use the service.

### Phase 1 — Partner-backed pilot
- Select one narrow use case and one authorised payment/settlement partner.
- Agree who performs onboarding, KYC, AML/CFT checks, safeguarding, settlement, refunds, complaints and reporting.
- Complete security, privacy, fraud, reconciliation and operational testing.
- Pilot with a small number of merchants and customers in a controlled area.
- Publish transparent fees, limits, service terms and customer support routes.

### Phase 2 — South African expansion
- Add more payment methods and merchant types only after the first pilot reconciles correctly.
- Add card issuing, bill payments, cash-agent access and recurring payments through the relevant partners.
- Build measurable reliability, fraud-loss, complaint-resolution and customer-outcome reporting.

### Phase 3 — African corridors
- Evaluate each country independently.
- Secure local regulatory advice, licensed partners, settlement arrangements, FX and data-protection compliance.
- Launch only corridors that pass legal, operational and commercial review.

### Phase 4 — Wider global connectivity
- Add international payment providers and schemes where commercial agreements and legal permissions allow.
- Develop partner APIs and institutional infrastructure capabilities.
- Pursue public-sector and development-finance collaborations through appropriate programmes and formal procurement/partnership channels.

## 6. Regulatory and institutional engagement

DUMA must not describe itself as a bank, deposit-taker, card issuer, remittance operator or licensed payment provider unless it has the relevant authorisation.

For South Africa, prepare a clear activity map and seek specialist legal advice on the applicable National Payment System, financial-sector, exchange-control, AML/CFT, consumer-protection, privacy and credit requirements. Consider early engagement with the Intergovernmental Fintech Working Group (IFWG) Regulatory Guidance Unit to understand the regulatory perimeter. A sandbox is a possible route to explore for a defined innovative use case; acceptance is not guaranteed.

Potential discussions with the South African Reserve Bank, FSCA, National Treasury, payment-system participants and other authorities should be evidence-led and framed around safe, interoperable financial access — not a claim that DUMA is already a bank.

The World Bank Group is a development institution, not a retail bank for DUMA wallet customers. Potential relevance is more likely through financial-inclusion programmes, government or regulator collaborations, research and ecosystem initiatives than a direct consumer-wallet partnership.

## 7. Minimum controls before real-money launch

- Written legal opinion and regulatory activity map
- Signed partner agreements and documented responsibilities
- Customer onboarding, KYC and risk-tiering
- AML/CFT controls, sanctions screening and transaction monitoring
- Safeguarding/custody and settlement design
- Reconciliation and exception handling
- Secure API architecture, encryption, secrets management and access controls
- Certified card-data handling; DUMA must not store raw card numbers or CVV
- Fraud operations, velocity limits and account recovery
- Complaints, refunds, reversals and dispute processes
- Privacy notices, consent, retention and incident response
- Business continuity, disaster recovery and service monitoring
- Clear pricing, terms, limits and customer disclosures

Never ask customers to type voucher PINs, cash-send PINs, OTPs, bank passwords, MoneyGram references or card CVV into a DUMA demo screen. Never credit a wallet from a screenshot or unverified payment reference.

## 8. Pilot scorecard

Track:
- Successful transaction rate and end-to-end settlement time
- Reconciliation breaks and unresolved exceptions
- Fraud attempts, confirmed fraud losses and account-takeover rates
- Complaints per 1,000 transactions and resolution time
- Refund and reversal completion time
- Merchant activation and repeat usage
- Customer understanding of fees and transaction status
- Access outcomes for cash-first, low-data and underserved users

## 9. Immediate next actions

1. Choose the first real-world pilot use case.
2. Draw the funds flow from payer to merchant/recipient, including every regulated partner.
3. Prepare a partner pack: product demo, architecture, controls, operating model, commercial model and pilot metrics.
4. Seek legal/regulatory guidance before accepting real funds.
5. Secure a partner letter of intent or pilot agreement before building live payment rails.

**North star:** DUMA should make payments easier to access, easier to understand and safer to use — while earning trust one correctly settled transaction at a time.
